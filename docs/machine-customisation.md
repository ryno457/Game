# Machine customisation

> Status: implemented, tested headlessly, wired into the prototype build bar.
> Run `godot --headless --path . --script tools/loadout_check.gd` — 31 checks.

The ask was "a very high degree of customisation for the machines so the player
can eventually field an artillery type of army or a melee type or a range type
or all three above mixed". This is how that works.

## The shape of it

Four resources and one resolver. Nothing else in the codebase knows what a
weapon is.

```
MachineChassis  a frame: body stats, a carry capacity, and a list of hardpoints
MachineHardpoint  one slot: what KIND of part it takes and how BIG
MachinePart     one bolt-on piece: mass, stat deltas, and (if armed) a weapon
MachineLoadout  a chassis plus the parts chosen for its hardpoints
MachineRules    the global knobs — armour curve, overload penalty, melee reach
                ↓
LoadoutResolver → MachineSpec   the numbers the sim actually fights with
```

`MachineSpec` is the only thing the simulation reads. A hand-tuned
`BuildOption` with flat numbers resolves to a spec too, so the sim has exactly
one shape to handle and the legacy Guard/Turret/Radar/Bulwark options keep
working untouched.

**Adding a weapon family is a data change.** A mortar is not a special case in
code: it is a part whose `family` is `ARTILLERY` and whose `min_range_m` is not
zero. That minimum range is the entire implementation of "artillery cannot
defend itself".

## What makes a role a role

| | reach | dps per mass | dead zone | needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Melee** | 1.6 m | highest (2.33) | none | to survive the walk in |
| **Ranged** | 9–22 m | middling (1.90) | none | nothing — the safe default |
| **Artillery** | 20–34 m | lowest (0.72) | 7–12 m | an escort *and* a spotter |
| **Mixed** | both bands | between | none | loses to each specialist |

Those numbers are asserted, not described. If a balance pass makes melee worse
per mass than direct fire, `loadout_check.gd` fails and says so — that is the
whole reason the tradeoffs are tests rather than a paragraph.

Two constraints give artillery real texture:

- **Its dead zone is uncoverable by itself.** A Mortar Walker cannot shoot
  anything inside seven metres. Whatever reaches it, reaches it.
- **It cannot see its own range.** It shells to 20 m and reveals nothing. The
  Watcher (26 m of radar, no weapon hardpoint at all) exists to close that gap,
  which makes an unarmed machine a load-bearing part of an artillery army.

## Constraints on a build

**Fit.** A part goes in a hardpoint only if the kind matches exactly and the
part is no larger than the slot. A railgun refused by a medium slot is an
error the resolver *reports* — it is never silently dropped.

**Capacity, not a wall.** Every frame has a `part_capacity`. Going over does
not fail the build; it slows the machine, down to a floor of 40% speed. The
Overgun in the catalogue is deliberately overloaded (34 parts on a 30 frame,
88% speed) so there is a worked example of the rule in the data.

That choice matters because everything travels with the module. A machine that
cannot keep station with the convoy is its own punishment — no separate penalty
was needed.

**Empty hardpoints are legal.** A half-kitted frame is cheaper and faster, and
that is a real choice rather than an error state.

## Armour

Armour is damage *reduction*, not hit points:

```
reduction = armour / (armour + armour_softening)   capped at max_damage_reduction
```

With `armour_softening = 40`, an Ablative Slab on a Bastion (42 armour) stops
51% of every bite. Reduction rather than HP means plating is worth more against
a swarm of small hits than against one big shell, which is the behaviour that
makes a Breaker able to stand somewhere a Skirmisher cannot.

## The catalogue

Generated by `tools/build_machine_data.gd`, not hand-written. Hand-writing
`.tres` is how `ext_resource` ids go wrong silently.

```
Skirmisher     Ranged     14 mass    45 hp   22.0 dps    9 m
Lancer         Ranged     26 mass   130 hp   49.3 dps   14 m
Vanguard       Mixed      25 mass   130 hp   60.3 dps    9 m
Breaker        Melee      27 mass   170 hp   62.8 dps  1.6 m
Mortar Walker  Artillery  23 mass   130 hp   16.7 dps   20 m   blind inside  7 m
Siege Battery  Artillery  42 mass   330 hp   22.6 dps   34 m   blind inside 12 m
Overgun        Ranged     46 mass   330 hp   60.0 dps   22 m   OVERLOADED
Watcher        Unarmed    21 mass   110 hp    0.0 dps    —     sees 26 m
```

4 chassis, 19 parts, 8 loadouts. Regenerate after editing that script:

```
godot --headless --path . --script tools/build_machine_data.gd
godot --headless --path . --script tools/loadout_check.gd
```

## Where the cost went

Mass is conserved — it is the module's body relocated, never consumed — so a
build cannot charge mass for churn. The price is **time**:

1. Mass leaves the module the instant the button is pressed. It shrinks now.
2. The machine spends `build_time_s` in the assembly queue and does not exist.
3. Scrapping a live machine leaves a **wreck where it stood**. Nothing is
   refunded until the drone flies out and hauls it home.

So repurposing an army is always available and never free: a player who rebuilds
every wave is not poorer, they are late, and their line has a hole in it while
they wait.

## Not built yet

- **No in-game loadout editor.** The player picks from eight pre-assembled
  machines in the build bar; the parts system underneath supports arbitrary
  combinations but there is no UI to assemble one. That is the next piece of
  work and it is a feel question — how much fiddling is fun on a phone — so it
  needs a decision before it gets built.
- **Splash damage is instant.** `_fire()` applies artillery splash the moment
  the shot goes off. No shell travel time, so no leading a moving target and no
  visible arc. Both matter to how artillery *reads* and neither is modelled.
- **No incidental terrain scarring from weapons yet.** CLAUDE.md warns that
  per-shot deformation can trap the player's own units. Nothing deforms ground
  on impact today, so that trap is still ahead, not behind.
- **Parts have no unlock gate.** Everything is available from the first minute,
  which almost certainly wants to change once missions exist.
